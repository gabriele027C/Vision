About the book
"Flash Boys" by Michael Lewis unveils the shocking truth
behind the U.S. stock market's inner workings, revealing how
it has been skillfully manipulated to favor insiders. In this
riveting bestseller, a brave group of Wall Street rebels—some
of whom sacrifice lucrative careers—join forces to uncover
and challenge the corrupt practices that undermine fairness in
trading. This compelling narrative not only exposes the hidden
machinations of Wall Street but also emphasizes the
implications for anyone with a stake in the financial markets,
making this an essential read for anyone involved in investing
or retirement planning.


<!-- PAGE 4 -->

About the author
Michael Lewis is a prominent American author and financial
journalist renowned for his insightful commentary on
business, finance, and economics. A contributing editor at
Vanity Fair since 2009, Lewis has captivated readers with his
nonfiction narratives exploring financial crises and behavioral
finance. Born in New Orleans, he graduated from Princeton
University with a degree in art history before launching a Wall
Street career as a bond salesman at Salomon Brothers in the
1980s. This experience inspired his debut book, *Liar's Poker*
(1989). He gained further acclaim with works such as
*Moneyball: The Art of Winning an Unfair Game* (2003) and
*The Blind Side: Evolution of a Game* (2006), both of which
were adapted into successful films. Lewis's 2010 book, *The
Big Short: Inside the Doomsday Machine*, along with its film
adaptation, solidified his status as a leading voice in financial
literature. His books have earned multiple awards, including
two Los Angeles Times Book Prizes, and many have topped
the New York Times Bestsellers List, including his latest
work, *Going Infinite* (2023).

HIDDEN IN PLAIN SIGHT
Overview of the Secret Line Construction
  
By the summer of 2009, a clandestine project was underway
involving the construction of a one-and-a-half-inch-wide
fiber optic line designed to connect a data center in Chicago
to a stock exchange in New Jersey. Hundreds of workers
toiled in secret, unaware of the specific purpose of the line,
except that it needed to be incredibly straight and discreet.
Workers' Perspective and Motivation
  
The workers, hired during an uncertain economic time, were
more focused on their tasks than the project's overarching
goals. Dan Spivey, a key figure in the project, noted that the
construction crews were baffled but simply accepted the job
without questioning the line's purpose.
Spivey's Realization of Trading Opportunities
  
Spivey, a former stockbroker and trader, understood that
trading speed was crucial in capitalizing on price


<!-- PAGE 9 -->

discrepancies between markets. He recognized that previous
telecom routes were inadequate for the needs of modern
trading, prompting him to explore faster, straighter paths for
data transmission.
Developing the Route
  
After mapping out a more efficient route through
Pennsylvania that circumvented geographical obstacles,
Spivey began to pursue permits and construction plans with
little understanding of prior telecom routes or their
limitations. He teamed up with a construction engineer, Steve
Williams, to initiate the laying of the fiber.
Funding the Project
  
With the support of Jim Barksdale, former CEO of Netscape,
Spivey secured the necessary funding to lay a complex line
stretching from Chicago to New Jersey. They hired
construction crews and faced various technical challenges
along the way, especially in rocky terrains.
Selling the Line


<!-- PAGE 10 -->

As the line neared completion in 2010, Spivey and his team
began strategizing how to market this unprecedented
infrastructure to Wall Street firms. They recognized the line's
value was tied to the scarcity of high-speed connections, and
after conducting market analysis, they set a price that was
significantly higher than existing telecom options.
Initial Reactions from Wall Street
  
The secrecy around the project added to its allure; Wall Street
firms were initially skeptical but intrigued. Spivey presented
detailed maps and construction evidence, overcoming
disbelief in his offering's validity. Eventually, the demand for
the service grew as trading firms recognized the necessity of
faster data transmission capabilities.
Challenges in Finalizing the Project
  
Despite the excitement, various technical and administrative
barriers postponed the project’s completion. Negotiating
easements and construction permissions proved difficult,
with potential partners initially rejecting offers to provide
access through their properties.

Conclusion and Impact of the Line
  
Successfully overcoming obstacles, the team completed the
line, which shortened round-trip travel time from Chicago to
New Jersey to 13 milliseconds. The implications of this
technological advancement significantly impacted trading
practices, as firms sought to leverage speed for competitive
advantage in financial markets. The ultimate question of the
line’s purpose remained, but its value became increasingly
apparent in the rapidly evolving landscape of high-speed
trading.

Introduction to Brad Katsuyama
  
Up until the financial collapse, Brad Katsuyama felt detached
from the chaotic U.S. financial system. Employed at the
Royal Bank of Canada (RBC), he believed his bank was
stable and virtuous, largely ignored on Wall Street. His move
to New York marked a stark contrast between the excesses of
American finance and the prudent values he knew in Canada.
Culture Shock in New York
  
Katsuyama was shocked by the American way of life,
particularly the rampant consumer debt and social disparities
he encountered. His efforts to help the homeless in New York
highlighted a lack of appreciation for such gestures compared
to his experiences in Canada.
Identity and Work Experience
  
As a minority in a predominantly white suburb, Brad had
navigated his identity without much attention to race.
Attending meetings meant to promote diversity at RBC only
intensified his feelings of alienation. He had always resisted
forces that sought to label or separate him. Brad’s career path

failed to meet expectations.
Emerging Market Confusion
  
Brad's difficulties intensified as market dynamics changed

and execution. As his relationship with the market

By 2008, Katsuyama's frustration with the evolving market

As they fine-tuned their new software, dubbed “Thor,” it

TRACKING THE PREDATOR
Marketable Weapon Against Predators
  
By the end of 2010, Brad Katsuyama and his team developed
a tool to protect investors from high-frequency trading (HFT)
firms, which were seen as market predators. Despite
interacting with over a hundred professionals from big Wall
Street banks, Brad found that most had limited understanding
of the HFT landscape.
Fear and Distrust in the System
  
Many bank employees expressed a deep-rooted fear and
distrust towards their institutions. A case study is John
Schwall, who started his career in a position viewed as
significant but grew disillusioned after witnessing ethical
breaches during the financial crisis and the subsequent


<!-- PAGE 24 -->

acquisition of Merrill Lynch by Bank of America.
Schwall's Transformation
  
After the crisis, Schwall's loyalty faltered, leading to a quest
for transparency within the financial system. He became
increasingly suspicious of HFTs and their practices, going so
far as to monitor trading activities within banks, uncovering
the profit-driven motives behind HFTs using proprietary
technology to gain unfair speed advantages in the market.
Regulation and its Loopholes
  
The Regulation National Market System (Reg NMS),
designed to enhance market fairness, inadvertently favored
HFTs by failing to specify the speed of market data
processing. This allowed HFTs to execute trades faster than
average investors, creating inequality and enabling
front-running tactics.
The Impact of Volatility
  
Schwall noticed that the trading activities of HFTs
(frequently profiting from volatility) led to increased stock


<!-- PAGE 25 -->

market instability. He hypothesized that this volatility arose
from front-running behavior and the technological advantage
held by HFT firms, creating a choppy market environment
detrimental to regular investors.
The Role of Dark Pools
  
Dark pools, or private exchanges operated by banks, became
a way for institutional investors to execute large orders
discreetly. However, they often had little protection against
HFTs, as the latter could exploit knowledge of buy orders
within these pools to front-run investors' trades.
Gates' Investigation
  
Rich Gates, a mutual fund manager, conducted his tests on
dark pools, demonstrating the systemic risks posed by HFTs
in real time. His findings confirmed an abundance of
predatory behavior across various dark pools, leading to a
shocking realization of the widespread nature of exploitation
in trading practices.
Brad’s Vision for a New Exchange

Faced with these market predicaments, Brad Katsuyama
contemplated creating a new stock exchange designed
explicitly to protect investors. He sought support from
influential figures in finance but was advised that for genuine
independence from Wall Street, he would have to pursue this
endeavor outside his current employment.
Schwall's Revelations
  
John Schwall's research through social networks revealed a
network of key players in HFT operations, comprising
mainly highly skilled individuals who were unaware of the
ethical implications of their work. His discoveries suggested
that loyalty was absent at multiple levels, complicating the
path to reforming the system.
Conclusion
  
The chapter details the complexity and intricacies of a
financial ecosystem prone to exploitation by faster,
technology-driven entities. As individuals like Brad and
Schwall attempted to confront and understand these issues,
they uncovered widespread dysfunction in regulatory
frameworks and the financial institutions that maintained


<!-- PAGE 27 -->

them, ultimately igniting a desire for significant changes
within the very foundations of the stock market.

Sergey's Journey to America
Sergey Aleynikov, a Russian immigrant, arrived in the

environmentally sourced.
Career Development in Technology
Starting with no money and limited English, Aleynikov
found a job in a medical center and eventually worked his
way up in tech roles, including at IDT, a telecom company.

His talent for programming led to a high-paying position at
Goldman Sachs, where he was recruited to optimize their
high-frequency trading systems.
Life at Goldman Sachs
Aleynikov found himself in a challenging work environment
filled with high pressure and intense competition. Despite his
programming skills, he felt detached from the financial
implications of his work, focused instead on technical
problems. Goldman Sachs was embroiled in ethical issues
and adapting to the increasingly complex and competitive
landscape of high-frequency trading.
Technical Challenges and Solutions
Aleynikov's main responsibilities involved improving
Goldman’s slow trading platform, characterized by outdated
code. He faced numerous engineering challenges, which he
tackled by decentralizing the system and rewriting core
components for speed. However, he recognized that
Goldman could never match the nimbleness of smaller
high-frequency firms due to its size and bureaucratic nature.

Ethics and Intellectual Property Concerns
Despite enjoying his work, Aleynikov grappled with

possessive attitude towards intellectual property, particularly
concerning open-source software. He found this
counterintuitive to the spirit of programming and
collaborative innovation.
The Arrest and Legal Troubles

Aleynikov mailed himself proprietary Goldman source code.

arrest. The investigation lacked depth, with the FBI agent
unfamiliar with high-frequency trading and computer coding.
Conclusion
Aleynikov's story highlights the intersection of technology
and finance, raising questions about ethics in high-frequency
trading, the treatment of programmers, and the complexities
of intellectual property in the age of rapid technological
advancement. Following his arrest, he remained largely

Overview of Sergey Aleynikov's Trial
  
Sergey Aleynikov’s trial in December 2010 highlighted the
insular nature of high-frequency trading (HFT), with few
knowledgeable witnesses. An expert witness, Benjamin Van
Vliet, lacked practical experience in HFT but gained
attention due to media interest and the flash crash event. His
testimony, deemed misinformed about the actual market
dynamics, reflected the jury's significant gaps in
understanding complex technology and trading algorithms.
Jury Composition and Understanding
  
The jury primarily comprised individuals with limited
technical backgrounds, leading to difficulties in grasping the
significance of the evidence presented. Key testimony from


<!-- PAGE 41 -->

Misha Malyshev, Aleynikov’s former employer, highlighted
the inapplicability of Goldman Sachs’ proprietary code to
Malyshev’s new firm, yet portions of the jury appeared
disengaged. Goldman Sachs employees' testimonies often
obscured the details, as they lacked a full grasp of the
technicalities at hand.
A Second Trial of Understanding
  
In response to the trial’s shortcomings, Michael Lewis
organized a private dinner with tech experts to re-evaluate
the case from an informed perspective. These technologists,
initially skeptical of Aleynikov, recognized his coding
prowess and the context of his actions. Conversations
revealed that Aleynikov's intent was more aligned with
technological curiosity than malicious theft.
Deciphering Aleynikov's Actions
  
The discussion shifted towards the small amount of code he
actually took and its relevance. The new jury analyzed
Aleynikov's lack of nefarious intent and the commonality of
practices in programming. They concluded that Aleynikov
acted more like someone preserving his work than a thief,


<!-- PAGE 42 -->

viewing code as analogous to a personal notebook rather than
proprietary data.
Goldman Sachs’ Motivations
  
The jury speculated about Goldman Sach’s motives in
prosecuting Aleynikov, particularly in an industry where
technological advantages are crucial. The management may
have sought to reinforce the perceived value of their
proprietary systems, fearing exposure of their reliance on
open-source materials, which could undermine their
competitiveness.
Aleynikov's Personal Perspective and Resilience
  
Aleynikov appeared remarkably at peace despite his
circumstances, expressing a nonchalant acceptance of his jail
time as a transformative ordeal. His composure baffled
observers, but he attributed it to an understanding of life
circumstances and a shift in personal values away from
material wealth.
